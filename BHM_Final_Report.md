## 3. The filtration process

1. **Inbox check.** For each organization, search Gmail for the domain. This catches prior outreach and live threads. It caught earlier outreach to several Ohio universities (2025), a live thread with a city, and a 2024 email to a corporate recipient.
2. **Pass 1 triage** (every row, fast). Drop a row if it has no contact path, is a duplicate, is already contacted, is Low fit with no evidence, has only a guessed inbox, has only a C-suite name, or is clearly defunct. For Ohio, drop a row if the center was closed under state law SB 1.
3. **Pass 2 verification** (High, then Medium; Low is held). Open the organization's own page. Confirm name, exact title, email and date. Check whether it accepts outside proposals. Record the source and date checked.
4. **Draft** only to addresses read on an opened page. Flag snippet-only addresses. CC a general inbox when the named email is snippet-only.
5. **Log** each org in one of three places: Outreach Log, No Email Found with a route to try, or a region triage tab.
6. **Report** rows in, dropped, High, Medium, Low held.

**Cost.** The West Coast pass took about 35 searches and about 30 page fetches to yield 17 drafts. Fast triage saved most of the effort. It also meant about 43 West Coast rows (High and Medium) were never researched past a first look.

## 4. How Gemini's information was used, and how useful it was

**Useful.**
- **The organization universe.** Most organizations were real, relevant, and correctly categorized. The tiering (university center, museum, association) matched the right buyers.
- **The fit rating.** High-fit rows produced nearly all the drafts. I used it as a prior for triage.
- **A few evidence hooks.** The best email hooks came from film-series and speaker-series evidence on a museum or archive page.
- **The "General inbox" column**, when it matched a real page. See the accuracy numbers below.

**Not reliable.**
- **Named contacts.** West Coast: all 149 rows said "not found" for the named contact. In earlier rounds, named contacts were frequently stale: someone who had died, left, been replaced, or belonged to a different organization.
- **Inboxes.** For the 17 West Coast organizations I drafted, Gemini's inbox exactly matched the real page in 9 of 17 (53%). In the other 8 it was wrong, for example a wrong prefix, a different mailbox, or a wrong domain. Several chapter domains (association chapters) did not exist. Anything labeled "inferred" or "careers@" was a dead end.
- **"Accepts outside proposals?"** Never verified on a page, in any round. One organization's own auto-reply showed the answer was effectively no.
- **Evidence of past BHM programming.** Often old, mis-attributed (sponsor credited as host), generic (a retail promo page, a travel article), or not BHM at all. I did not use it as proof in emails.
- **Tier 4 corporate HR/People.** Zero usable contacts in five lists.
- **Defunct or changed entities.** A museum closed for renovation, a company acquired, an agency renamed, a campus center closed under state law, and a chapter website hijacked with spam.
- **"Verified" labels.** Gemini marked lists as sourced and verified. Many contact and evidence claims did not hold up when I opened the pages. I did not count them precisely.
- **Padding.** Several tables ran 35 to 40 rows when about 15 were real candidates. Rows with "not found" in every field cost time.

## 5. Feedback to put into the Gemini prompt chats for the next batch

Paste these as additions to the standing prompt. They address each failure above.

1. **Source URL for every fact.** For every named person, inbox, "evidence" claim and "accepts proposals" claim, give the URL of the page it came from and that page's date. If you cannot give a URL, write "not found". Do not fill a cell from memory.
2. **No guessed emails.** Never infer an address from a pattern. Give only addresses printed on the organization's own website, with the page URL. Do not list generic `careers@` or `hr@` addresses.
3. **Give me the staff or contact page URL**, not just a name. A link to the staff directory or contact page lets me verify in one fetch.
4. **Ask for programming roles.** Target student activities, public programs, education, events or a center's own director. Give the exact title as written on the page. Skip executives and C-suite.
5. **Proposal intake.** Link the page that says the organization takes speaker, program or event requests. If there is none, write "no proposal page found". Do not answer "yes" without a link.
6. **Evidence rules.** Give the year, the host organization, free or paid, a link, and a short quote. Only events from the last 18 months. Exclude retail promotions, travel articles, social posts by third parties, and anything hosted by someone else.
7. **Entity check.** Say whether the organization is operating. Flag closed, renamed, merged or acquired organizations. Flag state laws that limit campus DEI or multicultural offices (Ohio SB 1 is one example; check the others in the region).
8. **Official names and domains.** Give the organization's official name and homepage URL. For chapters of national groups, link the national chapter-locator page. Do not invent domains.
9. **Fewer, better rows.** Give the 15 best candidates per tier per state, not 40. Drop Low-fit rows. Every row should have a verifiable contact route.
10. **Tier 4 (corporate HR).** Skip it, or change the target. A named HR contact with a published email is almost never findable by search. If we keep corporate, ask instead for company programs that already host outside speakers, with a link to the event page, and for the public DEI or community-engagement inbox.
11. **Phone as a fallback.** For organizations with no published email (several museums and cities), give the main programming phone number with its source.
12. **Don't say "verified".** Label everything "unverified, from [URL]". Claude verifies.
13. **Output format.** One CSV file per tier, with fixed columns and separate URL columns. Please send tables as files, not pasted prompts. Two Tier 1 tables never arrived because the prompt was pasted instead of the output.
14. **Exclude already-contacted organizations.** We can give Gemini the list of organizations already emailed (names only, no individuals) so it does not return them again.

## 6. Open items and risks

- **DMARC / sender authentication** (section 1). Fix before sending.
- **Send plan.** Spread the 40 drafts over Sunday and Monday rather than all at once.
- **Follow-ups.** Decide the cadence for the 25 already sent. About two weeks is a reasonable first guess.
- **Three bounced addresses** need replacements. Two organizations (a university student-success office and an art museum) are worth another route.
- **Held rows not researched.** About 43 West Coast rows and about 6 Midwest rows are held as "not verified" or "no draft". They can be worked later if the first sends do well.
- **State laws.** I screened Ohio for SB 1 but did not systematically check other states' DEI-office laws. Some Texas, Utah and Florida public campuses may be affected.
- **Missing table.** South Central Tier 1 (universities) was never received.
- **Claims in the email.** "Around the country" and "audiences have responded powerfully" are stated as you gave them. Have two or three named examples ready if a recipient asks.
- **Tracker location.** The repo holds only this report and an older tracker with named contacts from the first round. If the repo is public, remove or move that older file.

## 7. Suggested success metrics for the next round

- Share of rows whose inbox exactly matches the page (West Coast baseline: 53%).
- Bounce rate (baseline 12%).
