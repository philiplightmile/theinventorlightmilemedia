# The Inventor outreach: comprehensive findings (exported 2026-10-14)

Prepared for Philip Musey, Lightmile Media. This is a narrative summary of everything of note from the outreach work, written so it can be turned into an audio brief. Plain language, short sections, numbers spelled out where they matter.

## 1. The big picture

Philip Musey wrote and directed *The Inventor*, a 15-minute short film about Garrett Morgan, the Black inventor of a safety hood who helped rescue workers in the 1916 Cleveland Waterworks tunnel disaster. Around the film he built a 45 to 60 minute session: context on 1916 Cleveland, a screening, and a talkback about who gets credit for innovation and the obstacles Morgan overcame to turn a good idea into a business.

Over the past weeks there have been four streams of work: a first wave of Black History Month emails to schools, nonprofits and universities; a corporate outreach pilot for February 2027; a sales sheet and tracker; and now a new play aimed at sports organizations, starting with the Cleveland Browns.

## 2. Week one results: schools, nonprofits and universities

Philip sent 108 first-touch emails in the first week. About 101 were delivered and 12 humans replied, an overall reply rate of roughly 11.9 percent. By audience: university and college addresses did best at about 15.8 percent, nonprofit addresses about 6.7 percent, and other addresses about 25 percent on a small sample. The subject line "A little-known Black history story with a lesson for everyday life" earned all twelve replies. A different subject family, "A little-known story your teams can put to work," went to 27 utilities and nuclear organizations and earned no human replies, so it should not be treated as proven.

The most common objection was budget. Idaho's Black Community Alliance declined for budget reasons. Stanford's Black Community Services Center got a thank-you and a $299 license offer draft. The University of Oregon contact was answered with a draft. A repeatable process and a markdown playbook of these results were written and shared.

Two mistakes were caught along the way and corrected: Philip had already replied to one contact before a draft was suggested, and the Idaho in-person fee was recorded as $5,500 when Philip had actually quoted $7,500.

## 3. Email deliverability problem to fix

Diagnosis from public DNS records: Lightmile Media's domain has two DMARC records, no SPF record, and no Google DKIM key. This explained four of seven bounces from the earlier send. The recommended fix, to be done by Philip at his DNS provider (GoDaddy), is to delete the duplicate DMARC record, add an SPF record that includes Google's servers, and turn on Google DKIM signing. Philip waived the bounce-rate cap for now, but the fix is still recommended before any large send.

## 4. The corporate pilot

Goal: Black History Month 2027 outreach to corporate buyers, targeting Manager and Director level people in learning and development, HR and culture. The earlier 500 to 20,000 employee size floor came from the agent prompt and Philip removed it.

Sourcing: Cold web research found almost no printed inboxes, so Lusha was used. Fifty credits produced 23 revealed work emails. About 90 percent of reveal attempts were restricted for compliance reasons and were not charged or circumvented. Results skewed toward learning and development titles. Lusha does not return company size, so headcounts had to come from web research.

Research on the 23 contacts used four research agents. Evidence was thin. Only about six pages were opened across 23 companies and no dated Black History Month evidence was found for any of them. Positioning labels were mostly defaults, not findings.

Drafts: Twenty-two unsent Gmail drafts were created and labeled BHM-Corp/ToSend. Sixteen use the Black History Month version and six use a team resilience version. Subjects:
- Black History Month version: "A little-known Black history story with a lesson for everyday life."
- Resilience version: "A little-known story with a lesson in team resilience."

Both approved templates are saved in the repository file EMAIL_TEMPLATES.md. Philip insisted on exact wording and no invented claims. The Black History Month template keeps "more than 20 workers and rescuers" for the death count and ends with: "Would you be the right person to talk to about programming for Black History Month 2027?"

The pilot's A/B design is directional only. Four per arm cannot declare a winner. A real test needs about 40 delivered per arm. One flagged item: the contact at Enviva may have moved on, so verify before sending. CareFirst was not drafted because Philip emailed another CareFirst contact in February 2025.

## 5. The Cleveland Browns thread

On October 12, Philip wrote to Andrew Berry at the Browns through the Harvard alumni messaging platform, citing the Browns' Be The Solution video and offering a February student workshop in Cleveland. Andrew replied positively on October 14: "We love this idea." He copied Peter John-Baptiste, Chief Communications Officer, and Renee Harvey, Vice President of Community Impact and Foundation.

Philip then wrote to Peter and Renee asking for 30 minutes to talk and included the film link. As of October 14 there is no reply from Peter or Renee in the mailbox. This is an open conversation.

Philip called this a repeatable play. The pieces that seem to matter: a hook taken from the organization's own work, a student and youth framing about helping young Black students see themselves as entrepreneurs and innovators, a local tie to Garrett Morgan's Cleveland story, a low ask, and a warm referral to the community-impact owner. An open question is whether the message or the Harvard alumni platform earned the reply. Harvard connection is now only a bonus flag, never a requirement.

## 6. Sports outreach: decisions and process

Decisions from Philip: target students and youth; start with Cleveland teams and then all NFL teams; send from the Lightmile Media Gmail account; no cap on the number of notes; no price in first notes because the student workshop may be free and that is still undecided; each note should cite a specific program the organization already runs; open to traveling in February.

Process: build a universe list; research dated evidence of Black History Month, youth mentoring, education or entrepreneurship programming; identify the person who heads community impact, foundation or youth programs; tier the organizations; draft one note per organization with the opening line rewritten from the organization's own program; track results.

## 7. Round one: ten organizations

Round one covered the Cleveland Guardians, Cavaliers, Monsters and Charge, plus the Bengals, Steelers, Lions, Ravens, Bills and Colts. The research was thin: mostly search snippets and only two printed emails.

Two Gmail drafts were created, both unsent and labeled BHM-Sports. One goes to Raven Payne, Senior Manager of Community Impact and Engagement for the Rock Entertainment Group, who serves the Cavaliers, Monsters and Charge. It cites the Charge's Barbershop Banter program. The other goes to the Ravens' general contact inbox and asks to be routed to Heather Darney, Vice President of Community Relations and Executive Director of the Ravens Foundation. Text-only notes were written for the Steelers, Lions and Cavaliers. The Cavaliers note was held because it is the same community team as Raven Payne.

## 8. The deep NFL pass: all 31 other teams

Philip asked for much deeper research, so one agent per team was deployed for all 31 teams other than the Browns. Each had to open at least eight pages and could run up to fifteen searches. In practice most opened eight to fourteen pages but ran only two to ten searches, and many skipped Juneteenth, STEM or Inspire Change searches. So "none found" means not found in the searches run.

Main finding: Philip's hunch was right that nearly every team runs year-round youth programming and many fund urban or underserved youth through NFL Inspire Change grants. Dated Black History Month youth programming in 2025 or 2026 is thinner, and entrepreneurship is the rarest theme.

### Tier A: strongest hooks

**Chicago Bears.** The Black History Month series "Love, Legacy and the City" in February 2026 and another series in February 2025, a Black History Month Youth Leader Award, school visits for grades 6 to 12 through Youth Guidance, and a Dream Summit in October 2026 for about 70 students. Contact: Mallorie Sanders, DEI and social impact programs manager.

**Los Angeles Chargers.** Bolt Academy, a career and leadership program with Inglewood schools and Brotherhood Crusade for about 20 juniors and seniors, plus Black History Month outings in February 2024, 2025 and 2026. Contact seen: James Collins, Vice President of Cultural Programs and Development.

**San Francisco 49ers.** An Inspire Change Cultural Tour pairing high schoolers with Black entrepreneurs (December 2023), and 49ers EDU STEAM career events for Title I school students. Grantees include Kingmakers of Oakland and Hack the Hood. Contacts: Justin Prettyman, Executive Director of the 49ers Foundation, and Sofy Navarro, Director of Program Impact and Strategy.

**Las Vegas Raiders.** The Hip-Hop Entrepreneurship Program Academy, the Raiders' 2025 Inspire Change honoree, serves at-risk and underserved youth. This is the closest entrepreneurship match found. No 2025 or 2026 Black History Month item.

**Seattle Seahawks.** Inspire Change: Leadership Beyond the Field for about 300 Seattle high school juniors and seniors (event October 2, 2026) and Game Changers with Boys & Girls Clubs of King County (launched September 28, 2026). Contact: Mario Bailey, Vice President of Community Engagement and Legends. The 2026 cohorts are already underway.

**Los Angeles Rams.** STEAM school visits that schools can request through an online form, a Pathways to Success mentorship program, and a February 2025 Black History Month screening at Dymally High School with an essay contest. Contacts: Johnathan Franklin, senior director of social justice and football development, and Mia Tolliver.

### Tier B: good hook, weaker or older evidence

**Baltimore Ravens.** A Black History Month art and essay contest for grades 6 to 12 (2021, 2022, 2024, 2026) and a $20 million Boys & Girls Club in West Baltimore.

**Buffalo Bills.** A Black History Month Story Hour in its fourth year in 2026 for young children, Black Carpet events in February 2025 and 2026, and a Game Changer Scholarship for career and technical education students.

**Pittsburgh Steelers.** A Black History Month Living Museum with Pittsburgh Public Schools (February 12, year inferred as 2026), a similar event at Barrett Elementary on February 28, 2025, and Steelers STEM reaching more than 24,000 students since 2019. **Philadelphia Eagles.** The Entrepreneurs Game Plan with Firstrust Bank is a high school entrepreneurship curriculum with a pitch finale at the stadium, run through Science of Sport. No dated Black History Month item.

**Washington Commanders.** A Black History Month event at Kimball Elementary on February 20, 2026 with DC Central Kitchen (a lunch and book raffle), and a Youth Academy pilot at KIPP DC for 50 elementary students. Age and format are a mismatch.

**Indianapolis Colts.** The Breaking Barriers Inspire Change initiative and a partnership with the Center for Leadership Development, which serves African American youth in grades 4 to 12. Best route is through that center, not the team.

**Tampa Bay Buccaneers.** A Player-Student Mentoring Program for up to 100 high schoolers and the NFL YET Center Jackson Heights in East Tampa, led by Jamal Jefferson. Best route is the YET Center.

**Tennessee Titans.** ONE Community with Tennessee State University and a youth summer impact grant with ten grantees including the Nashville Urban Debate League. Contact: Johari Matthews, VP and Executive Director.

### Tier C: thin

Arizona Cardinals (a Latino Heritage Month series but no Black equivalent), Green Bay Packers, Cincinnati Bengals, and New York Jets (route through Year Up, the New York Urban League and Boys & Girls Clubs).

## 9. Hypotheses: where the workshop fits

1. Slot into an existing February series: Bears, Chargers, Bills, Ravens, Rams, Steelers, Commanders, Jaguars. The hook is Morgan's story as the entrepreneurship counterpart to their Black History Month content.
2. Attach to a career or entrepreneurship event for high schoolers: Raiders, 49ers, Eagles, Seahawks, Chargers, Lions Academy, Texans career days.
3. Go through a team-funded youth nonprofit: the Colts' Center for Leadership Development, the Buccaneers' YET Center, 49ers grantees, Jets partners. This means less approval friction than the team itself.
4. Propose a Black History Month equivalent of an existing heritage series: the Cardinals, Panthers and Saints each run Latino Heritage Month school visits. Weakest evidence, biggest ask.
5. Use a school-visit request form where one exists: the Rams STEAM form and the Titans' Take A Titan To School.

Biggest risk: no team page says it accepts outside workshops, most programs are sponsor-driven or player-led, and February 2027 calendars may already be set.
