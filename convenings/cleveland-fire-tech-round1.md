## 1. Method and weights

**Weights.** Default weights are kept: sponsor intent 40%, buyer value 30%, pair fit 20%, access 10%. I made two definitional changes.

1. **Access is not counted twice.** Buyer value uses the first four buyer criteria: purchasing power, active trigger, reference value, likelihood to attend. Accessibility to Philip is scored once, in the 10% access term.
2. **Unknowns are discounted, not zeroed.**
   - Sponsor intent (0 to 100) is the mean of the signals I could evidence, times 20, multiplied by `0.6 + 0.4 × (signals evidenced / 6)`.
   - Buyer value uses the same discount, with 4 criteria in place of 6.
   - Every table shows the raw score, the adjusted score and the evidence count. This stops a company with one good press release from outranking one I know more about.

**Pair total** = 0.4 × sponsor intent (adjusted) + 0.3 × buyer value (adjusted) + 0.2 × pair fit + 0.1 × (access × 20). Pair fit (0 to 100) is my judgment of the value exchange.

**Tiers** use intent, evidence coverage and whether there is a practical path for a solo founder. A high score alone does not make a tier A.
- **A** = adjusted intent of at least 60, at least 4 signals evidenced, and a plausible local path.
- **B** = real signals, but a large company, a weak local link, or a conflict.
- **C** = poor Cleveland fit or too little evidence.

## E. Conflicts and framing risks

**Competing sponsors in one room (pick one per row).**
- PPE: Fire-Dex vs MSA (Globe, Cairns) vs Lion.
- Records: First Due vs ESO.
- 911 and video: Axon (owns Carbyne) vs Motorola vs RapidSOS vs Prepared.
- New suppression and detection: Sonic vs Seneca vs Pano vs HEN.
- Apparatus: Rosenbauer vs Pierce vs Sutphen.

**Buyers who are sponsors' competitors.** None found among the scored buyers. Check each attendee's in-house build or open-bid status before invitations go out.

**Public-sector ethics.**
- About 14 of 17 recommended seats are public employees.
- The Ohio Ethics Commission has no fixed dollar threshold for a substantial gift, and vendor-paid meals, travel and lodging are covered by Advisory Opinion 89-014, which I did not open [33].
- Cleveland Division of Fire, suburban departments, Akron, Cleveland Water, the Airport System and the County are the highest-exposure attendees. MetroHealth is probably public as well.
- Cleveland Clinic EMS is the clean private seat.

**Framing risks: pairings that cast buyers as villains.**
- **Sonic with any public buyer (highest risk).** The film's arc is an inventor whose tool was not trusted until it was needed. Sonic is the inventor in the room, and fire chiefs become the gatekeepers who "didn't listen". Mitigation: frame buyers as people who carry the risk and decide what reaches the fireground, with no demo and no ask.
- **Cleveland Water as host or buyer.** The 1916 tunnel was a city waterworks project. Some accounts say the city refused to back Morgan's Carnegie nomination [34]. The Carnegie Hero Fund says it decided on risk, not race, and found no evidence that race was the reason [34][O]. Keep the film's claims inside that record.
- **Axon and SkyfireAI with Cleveland.** Police drones sit in an unresolved policy process [21]. A DFR pitch can read as surveillance.
- **Local 93 and the city in one room.** The union and the city are in labor relations. Keep the topic safety, not pay or staffing.
