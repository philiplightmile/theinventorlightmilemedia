# EXPERIMENTS

## Experiment 1: Tier 1 (ramp buyers) vs Tier 2 (volume and referral buyers)

Question: which tier turns a first email into a sold entry licence, and which produces more upgrade conversations?

Arms
- Tier 1: P1 cohort/leadership programs, P2 retreat/offsite planners, P3 university conveners, P4 staff PD planners.
- Tier 2: P5 service club/faith/community chairs, P6 libraries/museums/film societies/historical societies/makerspaces, P7 youth STEM and entrepreneurship programs.

Assignment: by persona, not randomized. Persona and tier are therefore confounded with each other, with org size, and with whether the contact is a role inbox or a form. Read results as directional, not causal.

Metrics (per tier)
1. Reply rate = replies of any kind / delivered. A bounce or auto-reply is not a reply.
2. Interested rate = replies expressing interest in the film or a conversation / delivered.
3. Licenses sold = completed entry licences / delivered. Track units sold, not revenue.
Secondary, record but do not decide on: referral names offered, quote granted, upgrade conversations opened.

Rules
- Directional until 40 delivered per tier. Do not compare, change copy or drop a tier before then.
- Count only delivered mail (exclude bounces). Count form-only submissions separately; do not mix them into email rates.
- Same subject-line family and same send window across tiers; only the persona hook line varies.
- Log per send: org, persona, tier, signal_level, email_type, send date, reply date, outcome.
- Report A/B/C signal levels side by side so signal quality is not mistaken for tier effect.
- Offer terms stay out of this file and out of first-touch copy until OFFER_ASSETS.md is complete.

Decision after 40 delivered per tier: keep both tiers if interested rate differs by under half; otherwise shift the next batch toward the better tier and note the confound.
