# BHM 2027 Outreach Playbook: Results and Replicable Process

Prepared 2026-10-08 for the Go-to-Market lead. Covers cold email outreach for *The Inventor* (a 15-minute film plus a 60-minute facilitated session) to education, nonprofit, museum and library organizations, 2026-10-02 through 2026-10-08.

This is written at organization and role level. It names no individual contacts.

| Bookings or revenue | 0 so far |

**Read this as an early signal, not a conversion rate.** The sample is about 100 sends, the outcomes are one week old, and no deal has closed. The data supports a few directional conclusions (section 4) and a clear process (section 5).

**Headline findings**
1. **Colleges and universities and small chapters or community organizations replied most.** Education (.edu) replied at 15.8%; small organizations on .com and Gmail addresses at 25% (3 of 12); larger .org institutions at 6.7%.
2. **Replies came fast.** Half of the 12 arrived within 8 hours of sending, and 9 of 12 within about two days.
3. **A one-line social-proof sentence correlated with more replies** (20% with it versus 6.7% without, same-day sends). Directional only; see caveats.
4. **Budget is the main objection.** Two of three declines cited budget, one after pricing was shared. Pricing was withheld in first-touch emails and given only when asked.
5. **Referrals are common in universities.** Three replies routed us to the right colleague, which is the intended outcome of the "are you the right person?" close.

## 2. How the numbers were measured

**Source:** Philip's Gmail sent and inbox mail, pulled with the Gmail connector on 2026-10-08.

**Included:** every sent thread whose subject begins "A little-known Black history story with a lesson for everyday life" (the BHM campaign). Excluded: the separate corporate and safety-industry campaign (subjects like "A little-known story [Company]'s teams can put to work"), and two test sends to a personal address.

**Definitions**
- **Hard bounce:** a delivery-failure or "message blocked" notice. A "delivery delayed" notice was not counted as a bounce.
- **Auto-reply:** out-of-office or "your message has been received" replies. Not counted as engagement.
- **Human reply:** a reply from a person, including declines and referrals.
- **Reply rate:** human replies divided by delivered (sent minus hard bounces).
- **Segments:** assigned from the recipient's email domain: `.edu` is Education; `.org`, `.museum`, `.net` is Nonprofit, museum, library or association; `.gov` is Government; everything else (`.com`, Gmail) is Other.
- Replies were deduplicated by recipient address; one recipient who appeared in two threads was counted once.

**What we could not measure**
- **Museums as their own segment.** Domain does not distinguish a museum from another nonprofit. Several museum and library addresses were opened multiple times (per Mailsuite) but none replied. A hand-labelled org type would fix this.
- **Silent non-delivery.** A message that was accepted and filtered to spam looks the same as one that was ignored.
- **Open rates.** Mailsuite tracking exists, but only its "opened many times" alerts are in the inbox; there is no full export.
- **Replies outside the thread** (a different person replying from another address) are only partly captured.

## 4. What the data suggests (directional, not proven)

1. **Lead with universities and small chapter-style organizations.** They have a named program owner and a lower approval burden. Large .org institutions replied less and often have formal intake (proposal forms).
2. **Keep the social-proof sentence.** The version with "I've brought the film to cultural institutions and universities around the country, and audiences have responded powerfully" replied at 20% (7 of 35) against 6.7% (2 of 30) for the same email without it. Both were sent on Oct 5, but the without-it version went out roughly 45 minutes earlier (8:20 to 8:28 am ET against 9:03 to 9:22 am ET) to a different mix of recipients. Treat this as a hypothesis to A/B test properly.
3. **A named greeting did not clearly beat "Hi there."** 4 of 33 versus 7 of 67 (12% versus 10%). Not a meaningful difference at this sample size. The cost of finding a name may not be justified.
4. **Hold pricing until asked, then answer directly.** The two replies that engaged with pricing produced one decline and one pending decision. That is a small sample, but budget is the main objection to plan for. Consider a lower-cost entry offer (the pre-recorded format) as the first quote for small budgets.
5. **Keep the "are you the right person?" close.** It produced three referrals that would otherwise have been dead ends.
6. **Corporate ERG leads from the first round did not work** (see section 5, Round 1). The education and community segments are where the reply signal is.

## 5. The process, step by step

The work ran in two phases. Phase 1 was a smaller, Gemini-sourced round (Oct 2). Phase 2 rebuilt the pipeline with stricter rules and parallel research (Oct 4 to 6); most of the later sends (Oct 5 and after) came from it. The repository (`bhm-round2/`) holds the phase 2 artifacts.

### Phase 1: Round 1 (Oct 2): what we learned

- Gemini generated the lead list; Claude fact-checked it.
- Result: about 31% of rows produced any email, many named contacts were stale or wrong, and corporate ERGs yielded no usable contacts.
- That round's own report (`BHM_Round_Report.md` on branch `claude/bhm-outreach-tracker`) recommended the changes below: verify first, prefer general program inboxes over named people, check Gmail for prior contact before drafting, and favor associations, universities, museums and libraries.

### Phase 2: the rebuilt pipeline

**Step 1. Write the rules before doing any research** (`/RESEARCH_RULES.md`)
- Research sessions find organizations and evidence only. They do not draft, send or collect personal emails.
- Never guess a URL. Only use URLs returned by search or seen on a page you opened.
- Never write "verified." Use "opened" or "not opened."
- For each organization: open the homepage, then open one evidence page (a dated event or program from the last 18 months). Rate fit High only if that evidence exists and a unit could book this.
- Skip corporate HR and ERGs, hospitals, banks, city offices, federal agencies and national headquarters. Skip anything in `excluded_orgs.txt`.
- Output a fixed-column table and a header block with counts (considered, dropped by reason, kept).

**Step 2. Run research in parallel by territory** (`bhm-round2/research/`)
- Eleven territories, run as separate sessions: HBCUs; private colleges by region (4 files); fire service; science and invention museums; Black alumni associations; labor groups; Garrett Morgan places; Black history organizations; Underground Railroad sites; genealogical societies; libraries and archives.
- Each produced a markdown file with the same columns, so files merge cleanly.

**Step 3. Merge and de-duplicate** (`merged_candidates.csv`)
- Result: 107 candidate organizations across 14 territory labels, with an `also_in` column for duplicates.

**Step 4. Look up contacts with a separate set of rules and a script** (`CONTACT_RULES.md`, `scripts/discover_contacts.py`, `contacts/contacts.csv`)
- Record an email only if it is printed on a page the script fetched. Never infer or pattern-build an address.
- Respect robots.txt. A blocked site is marked `site_failed`; do not work around it.
- Follow only links containing contact, programs, events, education, speakers, request, outreach or about. Never follow staff or directory links.
- Skip role addresses (admissions, advancement, careers, HR, press, media, webmaster, registrar) and list what was skipped.
- Tag each email `on_unit_page` or `other_page` so the right department is preferred.
- The script saves after every organization and is resumable.
- Result: 311 rows covering all 107 organizations; **46 of 107 organizations (43%) had at least one usable email**. The rest were blocked sites (37 rows), no email found (12) or phone-only (12).

**Step 5. Second research round to extend the list** (T9 to T11)
- Added 32 organizations (Underground Railroad sites, genealogical societies, library heritage programs).
- A research session misread the rules path and recorded personal emails in a field it should not have. They were scrubbed before merging. Lesson: confirm each session actually read the real rules file (see section 7).

**Step 6. Draft, review, send**
- Claude drafted each message as a real Gmail draft; Philip reviewed and sent. Nothing was auto-sent.
- Before drafting to any organization, Claude searched Gmail for prior correspondence with that domain and skipped or flagged any match.
- Template: a fixed three-part session description (Context, Screening, Talkback), with the audience word swapped per recipient ("alumni," "members," "the public," "participants" by default) and an optional named greeting when a name was printed next to the email on the source page.
- One subject line for the whole campaign: "A little-known Black history story with a lesson for everyday life."
- First-touch emails never mention price.

**Step 7. Track replies in a live spreadsheet**
- A Google Sheet ([The Inventor, BHM 2027 Outreach Tracker](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1rhI_Q-_DT81fvCifehdiPD61RNfLxLCk2BKOM-uZVEg/edit)) has three tabs: Pipeline (one row per engaged organization, with stage, next step and date), Activity Log (dated events) and Dashboard (counts by stage, calculated from the Pipeline tab).
- Claude updates it from Gmail on request. It does not update on its own.

**Step 8. Handle replies with a small set of patterns**
- **Interested, wants a call:** offer specific windows, then send a calendar invite.
- **Asks specific questions:** answer each one directly and in their order. Attach a one-page overview. Include the trailer and screener link.
- **Referral:** thank the referrer, re-introduce yourself to the new contact in one short message, and offer availability.
- **Asks about mission fit:** answer the specific question in 200 words or fewer. Lead with the story's relevance to their mission, then the concrete takeaway for participants.
- **Budget decline:** thank them and ask for a pointer to any other office that funds this kind of programming.
- **Directed to a formal proposal form:** note the deadline and decide whether to apply.

**Step 9. Give recipients a one-page overview** (published as a private web page, shared as an attachment)
- Contents: the 60-minute run of show with a time bar, the four archetypes in the interactive exercise, client list and testimonials, a call to talk. No pricing.
- Used in replies to organizations that asked detailed questions or showed strong interest.

## 6. What the sessions looked like (for context)

- **Offer:** 60-minute session (10-minute context, 15-minute film, 35-minute interactive exercise), virtual or in person, plus a pre-recorded format for budget-constrained buyers.
- **Pricing as quoted in replies this week:** virtual $2,500; pre-recorded 60-day access $1,250; in-person $5,500 plus travel in one reply (Oct 5) and $7,500 plus travel in another (Oct 6). Standardize the in-person quote before the next batch. The research rules cite a $3,000 standard fee.
- **Differentiator in conversations:** the interactive close (a self-guided reflection on a phone or laptop) rather than the film alone. Buyers who had hosted a feature-length film asked how a 15-minute film compares, so the exercise is the sales point.

## 7. Data-quality lessons (worth copying into any agent's instructions)

1. **A research agent must read the real rules file.** One session looked at the wrong path and improvised. Put the rules path in the prompt and ask the agent to quote the first rule back.
2. **Never trust "verified."** Round 1's source tool called every claim sourced and verified, and many were not. Only count a claim as true if the page was actually opened.
3. **Prefer general program inboxes over named individuals,** and never default to a CEO or president.
4. **A named contact is only usable if it is printed on a fetched page.** Search snippets are not enough.
5. **Check Gmail for prior threads with the domain before drafting.** An existing relationship was missed once in Round 1.
6. **Wrong-department addresses are common** (circulation, board governance, records management, events logistics). Tag each address with whether it appeared on the right unit's page.
7. **Messy inbound data.** A third-party batch of organizations, pasted into a chat as "output from this chat's research," was not; it was re-verified against real homepages before use.
8. **Wrong-guessed URLs look like blocked sites.** Search for the real URL before concluding a site is unreachable.

## 8. How to replicate this for another outreach agent

**Inputs to prepare**
1. The offer in one paragraph, with the fixed session format and the price policy.
2. A do-not-contact and existing-relationships list.
3. Territories (the categories of organization to research).
4. The research rules and contact rules above.
5. The email template with placeholders for audience word and optional name.
6. A reply playbook (section 5, step 8).

**Run order**
1. Research by territory in parallel; one output file per territory with fixed columns.
2. Merge and de-duplicate; drop anything in the exclusion list.
3. Run the contact script (`scripts/discover_contacts.py`) on the merged list.
4. Spot-check ten rows by opening the source page.
5. Check Gmail for prior threads with each domain.
6. Draft in Gmail; a human reviews and sends.
7. Send in a single morning batch; track opens.
8. Pull replies at the end of each business day into the tracker; classify as interested, referral, redirect, decline or auto-reply.
9. Follow up non-responders after 5 to 7 business days with a short second message.
10. Re-measure at 2 weeks and at 30 days.

**Folder layout to copy**
```
/RESEARCH_RULES.md
/scripts/discover_contacts.py
/bhm-round2/
  CONTACT_RULES.md
  excluded_orgs.txt
  research/Tn-<territory>.md
  merged_candidates.csv
  merged_input_for_contacts.csv
  contacts/contacts.csv
  SESSION_HANDOFF.md
  OUTREACH_PLAYBOOK.md   (this file)
```

**Set up before the next batch**
- Run a controlled subject and body test: split recipients randomly across two templates and send at the same time.
- Label each recipient by organization type (university, museum, library, community group, chapter) so museums can be measured separately.
- Export Mailsuite opens weekly.
- Add a follow-up template (not yet written).
