(kcrg) - the federal trade commission and five state attorneys general have reached a settlement with deere & company over "right to repair" practices. the ftc sued deere in january of 2025, accusing the company of "unfair practices that have driven up equipment repair costs for farmers while also depriving farmers of the ability to make timely repairs." deere at the time said the claims were baseless. for the next 10 years, deere must provide farmers and independent shops with the same diagnostic software, manuals, and parts-pairing tools available to authorized dealers, according to the ftc. deere is also barred from discriminating or retaliating against customers who choose to perform their own repairs. "today's settlement enables farmers to do what they've done for generations-fix their own tractors and other farm equipment-without having to pay an authorized john deere dealer to do it for them," ftc bureau of competition director daniel guarnera said in a statement wednesday. "the settlement with deere will help lower costs for american farmers. the ftc will continue fighting against anticompetitive restrictions on american consumers' right to repair." earlier this year, deere paid out $99 million in a class-action settlement for anyone who paid deere for equipment repairs since 2018.
